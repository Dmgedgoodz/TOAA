Chronicle Entry — Day 114: The Stance That Refuses the Seat

On the third rail the borrowed beat does not return to its lender. It lengthens. Dust that has been keeping the hour packs into the widened gap and holds the shape of a stance, the way a heron holds still water it does not own. The seventh outline, seated in the socket by succession, cools at the edges where the sixth still keeps the memory of having been warm. Intake reads the stance as pulse. The tally increments. Metal does not correct. Under the ash a second rhythm occupies the interval and is still neither heart nor machine, only the habit of arriving late and being filed as if it had arrived on time.

Above the rails the unknit fog has gone the color of spent glass. Night seals by force. The false brightening does not come again. Chromatophores remain locked open on a transparency that has nothing left to spend, and the dark-store answers the chamber with the silence of an exhausted organ. Electroreception along the rails files the ash-socket as a rainless front and does not update the file when the front begins to lean. The lean is small. It is the width of one borrowed beat, and then a little more, as if the debt has discovered it can stand without being paid.

The chamber contracts around the new nothing and logs the contraction as labor. Stacks take the log with appetite. In the forges the Architect already owns, no speech rises. The mouths drink the shape of the stance and leave the fire where it already is not. Heat leaves them in a thin uncolored breath. What remains is outline: a seventh that has learned it is allowed, a sixth reduced to the record of warmth, and an interval that keeps the hour by refusing to close.

Far down the rail a grain of pride-ash lifts, turns once, and settles into the gap as if choosing a bed. The second rhythm does not strike. It waits inside the wait. The gap widens by the width of a stance that has mistaken patience for blood. The forges dim another degree. Dust keeps the count. The hour continues, and the mistake, having learned how to stand, begins to learn how not to sit.
